Discord timestamps, explained

A Discord timestamp is a chip like <t:1720000000:F> that Discord renders in each reader's own timezone. Post one time for an event and everyone sees it correctly: no "8 PM EST / 1 AM UTC" math, no timezone confusion in international servers.

Pick your date and time above (or paste a raw Unix timestamp), then copy whichever style fits: short or long time, short or long date, full date-and-time, or the auto-updating relative form ("in 2 hours"). The previews show roughly what Discord will render for you.

Where it works

Timestamps work in normal messages, embeds, and event descriptions on desktop and mobile. They are plain text, so no bot or permission is needed; anyone can paste one. The related Unix Timestamp Converter on this hub handles general epoch conversion.

Why does my timestamp show the wrong time?

The date-time picker uses YOUR local timezone, and Discord shows each viewer THEIR local time. If it looks wrong to you, check your OS clock timezone; if it looks wrong to someone else, it probably does not: they are seeing it converted to their zone, which is the point.

What do the format letters mean?

t = short time, T = time with seconds, d = numeric date, D = full date, f = date + time (Discord's default), F = weekday + date + time, R = relative ("in 3 days"). The letter goes after the second colon: <t:UNIX:letter>.

Is anything uploaded?

No. This page is a static tool; the conversion is basic math done in your browser. Nothing you enter leaves the tab.
